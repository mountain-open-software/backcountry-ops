OPEN MOUNTAIN — AGREED DAILY PROCESS PLAN

Status: Product and architecture requirements, not a claim of implementation.

1. Trip and day structure
Communities contain groups and trips. Users can belong to multiple communities
and groups and hold trip-scoped leader roles. A single-day trip starts with one
OperationalDay; a multi-day trip starts with one for each date, including rest
days. Each OperationalDay owns its daily process, observations, and decisions.
Multi-day itinerary links resolve to their corresponding operational days.

2. One shared day, no group splits
A trip has exactly one OperationalDay per date. All participants continue
recording their own observations and events on that shared day. Group splitting
within a trip and same-date day copies are not supported. Separate operating
groups must use separate trips. This supersedes the earlier split-copy plan.

3. Morning briefing
Record the day's objective and strategic operating mindset, an ordered list
of plans (A, B, C onward), each plan's hazards, and conditions triggering
a plan change. Plans can be added, removed and reordered; saving a change
requires renewed approval. Existing A/B records remain readable.
Everyone, including the leader, records an individual assessment before the
group combines them. Retain differing views and unresolved concerns.

4. Avalanche assessment
For each avalanche problem, record type, terrain location, likelihood, and
expected destructive size/range, with source evidence and uncertainty.
Keep multiple problems separate. Use versioned vocabulary and confirm the
primary CMAH references and scale definitions before standards implementation.

5. Strategic mindset
Use the seven supplied core entries: Assessment, Stepping Out, Status Quo,
Stepping Back, Entrenchment, Open Season, and Spring Diurnal. Keep Maintenance
and High Alert identified as extensions. Record rationale, evidence, terrain
implications, and change triggers. Mindset is not mood or a safety guarantee.
Verify primary framework attribution and terminology before publication.

6. Human factors and privacy
All assessments are public within the authorized trip/group except each
person's own human-factor section. That section is private, including the
leader's. Consider readiness, comfort, fatigue, pressure, communication, and
bias without diagnosing participants. Separate group discussion contributions
from private reflections.
AI may read private summaries solely to assess human-bias/group-dynamics risk.
The resulting privacy-preserving risk indicator is visible to everyone.
No private quotations or identifiable explanations may leak through it.
Do not reuse private summaries for ordinary reports or general AI context.
Score format, thresholds, processing consent, and privacy implementation still
need definition. This indicator is not an overall mountain-safety judgment.

7. Combined proposal and approvals
AI drafts a combined proposal for everyone to review and correct. Every
participant must approve its exact version before it becomes accepted. One
phone may record an event attesting to the full group's explicit approval;
separate devices/signatures are not required. Record recorder, participants,
proposal/roster versions, and times. Missing responses are not approval.
Manual proposals remain possible without AI.
The leader may approve actions within the accepted plan and agreed triggers.
Going against what was agreed requires unanimous approval of the revision.
No vote or approval overrides a person's choice not to participate.

8. Voice-first and events
Voice-to-text is the primary interaction. Forms are optional for targeted
corrections. Input becomes validated commands and durable events; application
data is updated from those events. Record attribution, event time, recorded
time, revisions, and lineage. Support deterministic replay and idempotency.
Capture useful available context automatically: user, trip/day, time, and
permitted point location/accuracy. Missing or stale context remains explicit.
Save recordings offline even when transcription is queued. No continuous
ambient recording or hidden location tracking is implied.

9. Routes and tracks: external apps only
Open Mountain does not record live GPS tracks or provide in-app route drawing.
Participants use their own navigation/tracking apps. Planned routes and recorded
tracks enter Open Mountain only through file uploads or external links, such
as Strava, Outmap, or another provider. No provider-specific live API connection
is assumed. Preserve source, uploader, intended-versus-recorded distinction,
trip/day linkage, coordinates/times when present, and original files.
External links alone are not an offline route package. Imported files must
remain usable offline and exportable. Point GPS metadata on an observation is
distinct from continuous track recording and remains permission-dependent.

10. Field reporting
Each participant, including the leader, records and submits their own
observations. Saving alone is not submission. A person may explicitly submit
nothing to add; the leader does not silently submit on others' behalf.
At closing, AI summarizes submitted reports, preserving originals, sources,
differences, and uncertainty. Exclude private human-factor content. The closing
original and revised summaries are approved by the leader.

11. Debrief
Support individual debriefs, a team debrief recorded together on one phone,
or both. Record actual participants and recorder without overwriting personal
reflections. Cover expected versus observed conditions, planned versus actual
activity, human factors at play, what went well, and what needs to change.
The leader confirms the debrief is done.

12. Closing and later updates
The leader explicitly confirms day closing after all required individual
submissions and the debrief are done. Record a closing event; never close
automatically. The day can close offline while its AI summary is queued.
Different trips' days close independently.
Participants may add or correct observations afterward without reopening the
day. Show those events in the timeline at recorded time, with observation time
separately visible. Preserve originals. Mark outdated summaries and queue a
refresh; summary failure does not reopen the day.

13. Delivery boundary
The existing PWA is a local-only scaffold using forms and direct PouchDB writes.
Voice input, event-driven projections, protected personal assessments, group
approval, route/track uploads, external-link handling, AI, and closing workflow
remain planned. An API is not required for local operation. React Native,
SQLite, CouchDB/synchronization, and authenticated collaboration remain future
work as described in the roadmap and architecture documents.

14. Confirmed review resolutions
Adding an existing standalone day trip moves it into the expedition only after
explicit confirmation, preserving observations/history rather than copying.
Each user and community have separate databases, including local copies.
Private sections remain in the owning user database and work offline. Sync is
scoped by user ID/community membership. AI runs on the server; no PINs.
Local workflows remain usable without a backend. Remote authorization and
user/session/database mapping still require definition.
Post-close edits automatically amend the report without resubmission and queue
an updated AI summary. Mark it revised with a post-close-edit notice. Preserve
previous approved summaries and source report revisions. Generating a revision
does not approve it; the leader approves original and revised summaries.

15. Day-date conflicts and future merge
Offer merge when a destination expedition already has a day on that date,
but label it Coming soon. Merge is deferred, not implemented in the initial
scope. Leave both records unchanged and do not partially move or duplicate a
day. Non-conflicting moves still ask for confirmation.

16. API-free first version
The user sets their own local username profile; keep a stable internal user ID.
No remote login, API, AI server, or synchronization is required for local use.
Server AI is visibly not configured rather than presented as running. Manual
proposals and local capture remain available; missing AI does not block closing.
Version one assumes solo operation. Each user keeps an independent local copy
and manually retypes information where necessary. No synchronization or
shared-device collaboration is required. Server-backed collaboration comes
later. The sole local participant/leader records their own approval, submission,
debrief, and explicit closing; those do not act for users on other copies.

17. Next phase: collaboration
After solo local use, enable collaboration using CouchDB built-in users,
authentication, and database security. Users can then set passwords. Keep
private user databases and shared community databases separate, explicitly map
local profiles to authenticated accounts, and enforce role/event write rules
on the server. Version one remains password-free and requires no server.

18. Rendering and implementation update
Use normal React components with TypeScript and Vite for mobile and desktop.
Community/personal JSON view rendering is deferred for simplicity. The solo
event-driven workflow is now implemented locally; see solo-version-implementation.md
for exact coverage, recording/backup limits, and features still deferred.
Server AI, collaboration, synchronization, native builds, and merging remain
unavailable; this does not complete all Phase 0/1 roadmap gates.

19. Expo native companion
An Expo/React Native/TypeScript app now shares the solo command/event engine.
Native SQLite implements one database per user/community; local files retain
audio and GPX evidence. iOS/Android bundles export, but signed binaries and
actual device testing are not provided by this cloud workspace. No server, AI,
synchronization, JSON renderer, or day merging is introduced.
